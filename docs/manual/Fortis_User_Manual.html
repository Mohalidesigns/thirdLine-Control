§ 01 · Introduction

Introducing Fortis

Fortis is the second-line-of-defence platform in the Atheris family. It gives the internal control function one place to define controls, test them, rate them, and follow every failure through to closure that someone has verified.

The problem Fortis solves

Most control functions run on spreadsheets, email and memory. A checklist is ticked, an exception is raised in an email, a department promises a fix, and six months later nobody can show whether the fix was made or who checked it. An examiner asks for evidence and the team rebuilds it by hand.

Fortis replaces that with a recorded chain. Every control has an owner and a schedule. Every test produces a result and, where it fails, an exception. Every exception has a severity, a deadline and a named owner, and it can only close after the control function has verified the fix. Every step is logged with who did it and when.

Where Fortis sits

First line
Business owners

Run the business and operate controls day to day. In Fortis they are Control Owners: they remediate exceptions, upload evidence and answer the control function.

Second line · Fortis
Internal control

Designs, tests and monitors controls. Raises and tracks exceptions. Verifies remediation and closes exceptions. This is the team Fortis is built around.

Third line · thirdLine
Internal audit

Gives independent assurance. Fortis publishes approved controls, signed-off test results and closed exceptions to Atheris thirdLine so audit can rely on them.

Principles built into the product

  • Maker–checker. The person who creates a control, a test script, a rating or a mapping never approves it.
  • Only the control function closes. An exception closes only when a Control Function Head has verified the remediation. Owners cannot close their own exceptions, and the System Administrator has no bypass.
  • Nothing is silent. Every create, change, approval, closure and escalation is written to an audit trail that cannot be edited or deleted.
  • Rules are data, not code. Severities, deadlines, rating matrices, escalation ladders and risk scales are configured per organisation.
  • A person decides. Automated monitoring and AI suggestions prepare work. They never sign off a test, rate a control or close an exception.
§ 02 · Access

Signing in and account security

Fortis runs in a web browser. Your administrator gives you the address for your organisation's installation and creates your account.

2.1Signing in

  1. Open the Fortis address in a current version of Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari.
  2. If your organisation uses single sign-on, click your identity provider's button under or continue with (for example Microsoft Entra ID) and sign in with your work account. Fortis creates your profile on first sign-in and gives you the role your directory group maps to.
  3. Otherwise enter your work email address and password and click Sign in. Tick Keep me signed in only on a computer you alone use.
  4. If multi-factor authentication is required for your role, enter the six-digit code from your authenticator app.
  5. You land on the Dashboard.
Forgotten password

Click Forgot password? on the sign-in page and follow the emailed link. Where single sign-on is active, reset your password in your organisation's directory instead; Fortis does not hold it.

2.2Multi-factor authentication (MFA)

Your administrator decides which roles must use MFA. When it becomes mandatory for your role you get a grace period to enrol; after it ends, Fortis blocks access until you do.

  1. Open the user menu at the top right and choose Security & MFA.
  2. Scan the QR code with an authenticator app (Microsoft Authenticator, Google Authenticator or similar).
  3. Enter the code the app shows to confirm enrolment.
  4. Save your recovery codes somewhere safe. Each works once, if you lose your phone.

An emailed one-time code is available as a backup. SMS codes can be switched on by your administrator, but carry a SIM-swap risk and are not recommended.

2.3Break-glass access

When single sign-on is active, password sign-in is switched off for everyone except a small number of named break-glass accounts. These exist for the day the identity provider is unavailable. Their use is capped per day, always audited, and every use alerts all System Administrators.

2.5Working offline and on mobile

Fortis can be installed on a phone or desktop as an app from the browser menu. The My Tasks screen is designed for a phone, so a branch officer can see and work their checklist on the move. If the connection drops, a banner tells you, and Fortis shows an offline page rather than a broken screen.

4.3Separation of duties

Rule

These rules are enforced by the system for every user, including the System Administrator:

  • The maker of a control, test script, rating, compensating control, mapping or monitoring rule cannot approve it.
  • The tester of a test cannot review it.
  • Only a Control Function Head can close an exception, only from Remediated, and never when they ran the source test, own the failed control or own the exception.
  • The person who raised an exception cannot submit the department's response to it, and the person who responded cannot review that response.
  • Risk acceptance needs a Control Function Head and cannot be granted by the control's owner.
  • Whoever generates a regulatory submission pack cannot approve it; whoever approves cannot file it.

If a button you expect is missing on a record, the most likely reason is one of these rules. Ask a colleague who is not connected to the record to take the step.

§ 05 · Structure

Control Structure

The Control Structure is the front door for the control function. It organises everything the second line oversees into sub-units and control entities, the same way an audit universe organises what internal audit covers.

5.1Sub-units

Open Control Structure › Control Structure. You see one card per sub-unit, each with its count of entities, attached controls, open exceptions and overdue reviews. Three sub-units are provided:

HOC
Head Office Control

The head-office departments the control function oversees: treasury, trade, HCM, general services and so on.

ISC
Information Systems Control

The IS domains: access management, change management, ATM and card systems, e-channels, data centre.

BRC
Branch Control

Every branch, each with the same set of branch activities drawn from a template.

Your organisation can add sub-units, for example Subsidiary Control, with New sub-unit. Each sub-unit has a domain that decides how it behaves; its name is only a label.

5.2Control entities

A control entity (reference CE-…) is one thing the second line oversees: a department, an IS domain, a branch or a branch activity. Open a sub-unit to see its register, then New entity to add one. Each entity records:

  • the organisation unit it corresponds to (required for branch entities);
  • its owner and risk rating;
  • its review cadence, so overdue reviews show on the sub-unit card.

The entity profile has tabs for the Controls attached to it, its Exceptions and its Tests.

5.3Branches are provisioned automatically

You do not maintain a list of branches in Fortis. Branch Control reads its branches from your organisation units of type Branch. When a branch is created, it appears under Branch Control at once, with every active branch activity template already in place beneath it — Cash Management, Teller Operations, Vault, ATM, POS, Customer Account Opening, KYC, Funds Transfer, Clearing & Settlements and E-Business Channels.

To change the template, open Branch Control and use Add template activity. A new template activity reaches existing branches on the next nightly sync. Editing or deleting a template never changes activities already created under a branch.

5.4Attaching controls

  1. Open the control entity.
  2. Click Attach controls, search the library and select one or more controls.
  3. Tick Key for a control the entity relies on for a material risk.
  4. Click Attach.

A control may sit under many entities. An ATM control, for example, can sit under Information Systems Control and under every branch.

Rule

You cannot detach a control from an entity while it has open exceptions or tests in progress. Deactivate the entity instead.

5.5Stakeholders on a control

A control can involve more than one unit. On the control's page, the Stakeholders panel lists each unit's part: owner, co_owner, contributor or consulted, with an optional named contact. There is always exactly one owner, and it matches the control's owning unit. Shared controls are badged Shared in other units' registers. When an exception is raised on a shared control, co-owner unit heads and named contacts are notified automatically.

§ 06 · Library

The Control Library

The Control Library is the register of every control your organisation relies on. A control becomes something Fortis can test only once it has been approved.

6.1Creating a control

  1. Open Controls › Control Library and click Add Control. To start from a standard control, open Starter library instead and copy one.
  2. Complete Control Details: a clear title (for example Segregation of Duties – Treasury), the objective (what outcome must this control guarantee?), the description and the control documentation it relies on. The reference is assigned automatically.
  3. Complete Classification using the values below.
  4. Set the Assessment Schedule: how often the control operates and how often it is tested.
  5. Set Ownership: the owning unit, department and control owner.
  6. Click save. The control is created as Draft.
  7. Click Submit for approval. A Control Function Head who did not create it then clicks Approve, or Return to draft with a reason.

6.2The control lifecycle

Draft→Pending Approval→Active→Under Review→Retired

Pending Approval can return to Draft. Under Review returns to Pending Approval or Active. Retired is final.

Only Active controls are tested and count towards coverage and residual risk. When a control is approved, Fortis publishes it to thirdLine, if your organisation has connected it. To change an approved control, click Edit; you will be asked why. The change goes back through approval, and the earlier version is kept in Version history with its effective dates.

6.3The control page

A control's page brings together everything known about it:

  • Effectiveness Summary — the current design, operating and overall ratings.
  • Recent tests — with Build test script if none exists yet.
  • Open exceptions, Linked Risks with their inherent and residual ratings, and Known improvements.
  • Control structure and Stakeholders — where the control is attached and who shares it.
  • Assessment History — design and operating assessments over time.
  • Implicated in investigations — visible only to those allowed to see the investigation.

6.4Group controls and distribution

A group can define a control once and distribute it to its entities. Open an active group control and use Distribution to select entities. Fortis creates one entity copy per entity, each with its own owner, testing and rating, and tracks progress on an organisation tree: entities distributed to, tasks completed, outstanding, and implementation percentage.

  • When the group control changes, you preview the change and choose to propagate it or only notify entities. A field an entity has adapted locally is never overwritten without notice.
  • An entity that declines a distributed control must give a reason, and a Control Function Head must approve the decline. A declined control still reports as a coverage gap.
§ 07 · Scripts

Test scripts

A test script is the reusable checklist a tester follows. Write it once; every scheduled test of the control uses it.

7.1Building a script

  1. Open Controls › Test Scripts and click New script, or click Build test script on a control.
  2. Choose the control, then write the objective and any sampling guidance for the tester.
  3. Click Add item for each check. Write each item as a question that can be answered Pass or Fail, for example “Was each reversal above ₦5m authorised by a second officer?”
  4. Tick Mandatory on items that must be answered, and set the severity on fail: the severity of the exception Fortis raises if the item fails.
  5. Click Submit for approval. A Control Function Head approves it before it can be used.
Tip

Set severity on fail carefully. It decides the exception's default deadline (Chapter 13) and how fast it escalates. A missing signature on a low-value form is not a Critical.

Scripts are versioned. Editing an approved script creates a new version, and tests already in progress keep the version they started with.

§ 08 · Testing

Control testing and rating

A test instance is one test of one control for one period. Fortis creates them automatically from each active control's test frequency, every night, so the testing plan fills itself.

8.1The test lifecycle

Scheduled→In Progress→Submitted→Reviewed→Reopened

A reviewer can return a Submitted test to In Progress. A Reviewed test is locked; it can only be formally reopened, with a reason.

8.2Performing a test

  1. Open Controls › Control Testing, or your item in My Tasks. Overdue tests are marked Overdue.
  2. Click Start test. The status moves to In Progress.
  3. Record Sampling: the population size, the sample size, the method (for example random sampling) and the references of the sampled items.
  4. Work through the checklist. Mark each item Pass, Fail or N/A. A comment is required for a fail or N/A.
  5. Attach evidence to the test (Chapter 23).
  6. Click Submit for review.
Rule

When a test is submitted, every failed checklist item raises an exception automatically, with the severity set on the script and the control's owner as its owner. You do not need to raise them by hand, and you cannot submit a fail without one.

8.3Reviewing a test

A reviewer who did not perform the test opens it and either clicks Sign off or Return to tester with review notes. Sign-off moves the test to Reviewed and publishes the result to thirdLine where connected.

To correct a reviewed test, click Reopen and give the reason. The reason is kept on the record.

8.4Rating effectiveness

After sign-off, click Rate effectiveness. Design and operating effectiveness are rated separately:

Design
Is it built right?

Adequate — the control is properly designed.
Inadequate — the design has gaps.

Operating
Does it work in practice?

Effective — operating as designed.
Partially Effective — some exceptions noted.
Ineffective — not operating as designed.

The overall rating is derived from the two through your organisation's rating matrix. By default, a control whose design is inadequate cannot be rated better than Partially Effective overall, however well it operates. A second person clicks Approve rating before the rating takes effect.

You can also record a design or operating assessment directly on the control with Assess Control.

8.5How ratings move residual risk

Fortis recalculates the residual risk of every linked risk when a rating changes. Each active control, and each approved active compensating control, reduces the inherent risk by a factor:

Overall ratingReduction
Effective50%
Partially Effective25%
Ineffective / Not Tested0%

The reductions are averaged across the risk's controls. Residual risk never falls below 20% of inherent risk.

§ 09 · Checklists

Control functions and checklists

Your departmental control function checklists — the daily, weekly and monthly checks each desk and branch performs — live in Fortis as control functions. The Frequency of Activity on each line creates the work.

9.1Importing the checklist workbook

The checklist is maintained in Excel with the columns S/N · Units · Function · Checklist · Frequency of Activity (Head Office) or S/N · Units · Desk / Role · Checklist · Frequency of Activity (Branch).

  1. Open Control Structure › Checklist Import, choose the workbook and click Stage a dry run.
  2. Fortis performs a dry run. Nothing is written yet. Review the result per unit: functions and lines added, changed, unchanged and unresolved. A row whose frequency Fortis does not recognise blocks the import — add an alias for that wording first. Fortis never guesses a frequency.
  3. If the result is right, click Commit the import. If not, click Discard, correct the workbook and upload again.

Every import is kept in Import history with its status: Dry Run, Committed, Failed or Discarded. A changed checklist creates a new version of the function; the previous version stays readable.

9.2Working a checklist task

Each night Fortis creates a task for every function, desk or branch, and period that is due. A daily branch function in 60 branches produces 60 tasks a day. The officer opens the task from My Tasks and records a result against each checklist line. A failed line raises an exception, exactly as a failed test item does.

Event-driven lines have no schedule. Open the function and use Trigger an occurrence to raise the task when the event happens — for example, when a vault custodian changes.

A function's page shows its checklist lines, the desks and branches that execute it, its versions and its full task history.

9.3Frequency Compliance

Control Structure › Frequency Compliance shows whether the work was done on time: completion by sub-unit and by function, over a date range you choose. Use it to see which desks and branches are falling behind before the gap becomes an audit finding.

§ 10 · Spot checks

Spot checks

A spot check is a targeted review of a unit or process outside the testing plan, often unannounced. It produces findings and an issued report.

Draft→In Progress→Completed→Report Issued
  1. Open Controls › Spot Checks and click New spot check. Set the unit, process and scope, choose the controls in scope, tick Unannounced if it is, and click Start spot check.
  2. During the check, click Capture finding for each issue, with its severity and evidence. A finding raises an exception against the control.
  3. Click Complete check when fieldwork is finished.
  4. Click Issue report. Download it with Download report (PDF).
§ 11 · Campaigns

Campaigns, attestations and surveys

One campaign engine runs three kinds of outreach to the first line: control self-assessments (CSA), attestations and surveys.

11.1Running a campaign

  1. Open Controls › Campaigns and create a campaign. Choose its type, the controls or units it covers, the respondents and the deadline.
  2. Build the questionnaire: sections, questions, weights, conditions that show a question only when an earlier answer calls for it, and answers that raise an exception.
  3. Submit it. A second person approves the campaign before it opens.
  4. Respondents receive it in My Tasks. A live gauge shows the response rate.
  5. A reviewer who did not respond reviews each response. Responses that differ from the campaign's expected score by more than its threshold are flagged.
Rule

A self-assessment never rates a control by itself. Its result is a proposed design rating, applied only when a Control Function Head accepts it.

11.2Attestations

An attestation asks a named person to confirm a statement, for example that they have read a policy. Fortis keeps the exact text they signed, with the time, IP address and method. Editing the source later does not change what was signed. Missing attestations escalate through the escalation matrix. See Controls › Attestations.

11.3Anonymous surveys

A survey marked anonymous stores no respondent, entity or IP address, and nobody — including administrators — can open an individual response. Only totals are shown.

§ 12 · Monitoring

Continuous monitoring and SoD

Continuous monitoring tests controls against data from your core systems every night, so a control failure is found in a day rather than at the next quarterly test.

12.1How monitoring works

  • Data sources — connections to core banking (Finacle, Flexcube, T24, BankOne), ERP (SAP, Dynamics 365, Sage), NIBSS, FIRS and generic REST, SQL, SFTP-CSV or manual upload — extract the night's data at 04:00. Sensitive personal data is hashed on the way in unless your DPO decides otherwise. Administrators set these up (Chapter 26).
  • Monitoring rules run at 04:30 against that data. A rule describes what a breach looks like — for example, a payment above a limit with no second approver.
  • Each hit becomes a monitoring finding, raises an exception, and creates the linked control's test instance.
Rule

A monitoring rule cannot activate on one person's say-so: its author and its owner cannot approve it. Changing what an active rule tests withdraws its approval. Automated results stop at Submitted — a person still reviews the test and a second person still approves the rating.

Sampling inside a rule is reproducible: the seed is stored with each run, so the same sample can be drawn again for an examiner.

12.2Segregation of Duties analysis

Controls › Segregation of Duties lists users whose combined system access breaks your SoD rules, such as one person able to both create and approve a payment. Each violation can be remediated, which raises an exception, or accepted by a Control Function Head with a mandatory expiry date. When the acceptance expires, the violation returns.

§ 13 · Exceptions

Exceptions

An exception is a recorded control failure. It has a severity, an owner, a target closure date and an age, and it stays open until the control function has verified that it is fixed.

13.1Where exceptions come from

Most exceptions are raised for you. Fortis raises one automatically from:

  • a failed checklist item on a submitted test (Chapter 8) or a failed line on a checklist task (Chapter 9);
  • a spot check finding (Chapter 10);
  • a continuous monitoring hit or an SoD violation (Chapter 12);
  • a CSA answer set to trigger one (Chapter 11);
  • an incident that names a failed control, a KRI breach, a lapsed third-party review or an assurance gap (Part IV);
  • an internal audit finding sent in from thirdLine.

To raise one by hand, open Exceptions & Issues › Exceptions, click Raise exception, and complete the title, control, severity, description, root cause if known, owner, responsible party, unit and target closure date.

A new exception is Assigned if its control has an owner, otherwise Open.

13.2Severity and default deadlines

SeverityDefault target closureTypical use
Critical7 daysLoss is occurring or imminent; a regulatory breach.
High14 daysA key control is not operating.
Medium30 daysA control is partly operating; compensating measures exist.
Low60 daysDocumentation or procedural lapse with no direct exposure.

Fortis recalculates every exception's age and overdue flag each night. An exception past its target closure date is marked Overdue. When the same control fails again in a later period, the new exception is linked to the earlier one and marked Recurring.

13.3The exception lifecycle

Open→Assigned→In Progress→Remediated→Verified-Closed
Open · Assigned · In Progress→Risk Accepted

A Remediated exception that fails verification goes back to In Progress. An expired risk acceptance returns the exception to In Progress automatically.

13.4Working an exception

Open the exception. The top of the page shows the control, risk, owner, responsible party, unit, who raised it, the target closure date, the number of extensions, and any earlier exception it recurs from. The actions available depend on your role and the status.

ActionWhoWhat it does
AssignControl functionNames the person responsible for the fix (Assign responsibility).
Post (Activity)Anyone on the recordAdds a time-stamped update. Use it for every material step.
Mark remediatedOwner or control functionStates the fix is done. Attach the evidence first.
Request extensionOwner or control functionAsks for a later due date, with a reason.
Decide extensionControl Function HeadApproves or declines the request and records the decision.
Verify and closeControl Function HeadRecords how the fix was verified and closes the exception.
Fail verificationControl Function HeadReturns it to In Progress, with the reason.
Accept riskControl Function HeadAccepts the residual risk until a mandatory expiry date.
Escalate to departmentControl functionIssues it formally through the Exception Manager (Chapter 14).
Register compensating controlControl functionRecords an alternative control covering the gap (Chapter 16).
Escalate to investigationControl functionOpens an investigation linked to the exception (Chapter 21).

13.5Verifying and closing

  1. Open a Remediated exception from the Dashboard's Pending Verification tile.
  2. Review the evidence and the activity thread. Re-perform the control if you need to.
  3. Click Verify and close. In verification method, write what you did, for example “Re-performed the control on the current period's sample”, and add closure notes.
  4. Click Verify & close. The exception moves to Verified-Closed, with your name and the time, and is published to thirdLine where connected.
Rule

Only a Control Function Head can verify and close, and only from Remediated. They cannot close an exception they own, one on a control they own, or one raised from a test they performed. If any of the exception's departmental escalations is still open, it cannot close at all. There is no administrator override.

13.6Accepting risk

Where the business decides not to fix a failure, a Control Function Head who does not own the control can click Accept risk. An expiry date is mandatory. On that date Fortis reopens the exception automatically so the decision is looked at again. Accepting risk withdraws every open departmental escalation on the exception, with the acceptance as the reason.

§ 14 · Exception Manager

The Exception Manager

The Exception Manager turns an exception into a formal, addressed notice. The control function issues it to the departments responsible, each department answers on the record, and the answer is reviewed round by round until the fix is validated.

14.1The loop

Issue→Acknowledge→Respond→Review→Track actions→Validate→Closed

A rejected response, or a validation that is not Effective, re-issues the escalation as a new round with a fresh deadline.

Each department you issue to gets its own escalation with its own reference (EXE-…), respondent, deadline and response thread. An exception issued to Operations and IT has two escalations.

14.2Issuing an exception

  1. Open the exception and click Escalate to department.
  2. Choose who it goes to: a Department / unit, a Business process, a Named user or an External processor. Click Add another department to issue to more than one.
  3. Leave the respondent on Resolve automatically, or name one. Fortis finds the respondent from your routing rules, then the unit head, then the process owner. If it cannot find anyone, it stops and tells you; it never issues to nobody.
  4. Choose what you need back: Root cause + action plan, Root cause only, Action plan only or Confirmation only.
  5. Write what the control function is asking for and send. The deadlines come from the SLA policy for the exception's severity.

14.3Responding — for departments

The respondent sees the escalation in My Tasks and by email. Opening the response screen for the first time records the acknowledgement. A department cannot later say it never saw the notice.

  1. Open the escalation and click Respond. Read what is being asked of your department.
  2. Choose your position: Accepted, Partially Accepted, Disputed, Already Remediated or More Information Required.
  3. Write the management comment. If you dispute the exception, this becomes your rebuttal: explain why.
  4. Give the root cause and its category — people, process, technology, governance or external — and the agreed action plan with a proposed target date, where they were asked for.
  5. Attach evidence. Click Save draft to come back later, or Submit response.

14.4Reviewing a response

A member of the control function who did not write the response clicks Review response:

  • Accept — the response stands. The action plan becomes a tracked action (EXA-…) owned by the department. Only a holder of the closing authority can accept.
  • Reject — re-issue for a new round. The escalation goes back to the department as round two, three and so on, with a fresh deadline. Earlier rounds stay on the record and cannot be edited.

14.5Tracking and validating actions

The department records progress against each committed action with Record progress and Mark completed, attaching evidence. The control function then validates the remediation:

ValidationResult
EffectiveCloses the escalation.
Partially EffectiveRe-issues the escalation for another round.
IneffectiveRe-issues the escalation for another round.

An escalation issued in error can be withdrawn with Withdraw. A reason is mandatory and recorded. The exception itself can reach Verified-Closed only when every escalation on it is Closed or Withdrawn.

14.6External processors

A third party with no Fortis account answers through a secure link. On the escalation, click Generate link. The link works for that one escalation only, expires with the response deadline unless you set another date, can be revoked at any time, and records every visit with IP address and device. It is shown once; copy it into your message to the processor.

14.7The register and its clocks

Exceptions & Issues › Exception Manager lists every escalation, with a department scorecard and an ageing view, and exports to Excel and PDF, including a board and audit committee extract. Each escalation shows when it was issued, acknowledged and responded to, the SLA policy, reminders sent and whether it has reached the escalation matrix. Items are marked Late or Overdue.

Deadlines are counted in business days in your timezone and skip public holidays. Every morning at 06:30 Fortis sends the reminders each policy calls for, marks responses overdue, and passes unanswered escalations to the escalation matrix (Chapter 15).

Rule

The person who issued the exception cannot submit the department's response. The respondent cannot review it. The System Administrator configures routing and SLA policies but takes no part in the loop.

§ 15 · Escalation

Escalation and routing

Escalation makes sure an ignored exception does not stay ignored. It runs every morning at 07:00 and moves overdue items up a ladder your organisation defines.

15.1The escalation matrix

Exceptions & Issues › Escalation Matrix (System Administrator and Control Function Head) defines, for each severity, who is notified and after how long. A typical ladder:

TierRecipientExample trigger
1Line Manager of the ownerCritical exception overdue by 1 day; High by 3 days.
2Control Function Head and department headStill overdue a week later.
3Executive Viewer (executive or board committee)Still overdue a further two weeks.

Besides overdue exceptions, the matrix handles departmental escalations that are unacknowledged, overdue or rejected, overdue attestations, risk appetite breaches and overdue treatment plans. Each item escalates once per tier; running the job twice never sends the same notice twice.

15.2Exception routing and SLA policies

Exceptions & Issues › Exception Routing holds rules that pre-fill the recipient when an exception is issued, for example ATM exceptions go to E-Business. The first matching rule wins, and it only pre-fills the form; the issuer can change it.

SLA policies, one per severity, set the hours to acknowledge, the business days to respond, when reminders are sent, the maximum number of reminders, and when an unanswered escalation passes to the matrix.

15.3Owner digests

Every Monday at 07:30, each control owner receives a digest of their open and overdue exceptions.

§ 16 · Mitigation

Compensating controls and improvements

16.1Compensating controls

A compensating control is an alternative control that covers a weakness while the primary control is being fixed — for example, a daily manual review of reversals while a system authorisation limit is corrected.

  1. On the exception, click Register compensating control. Describe the control, name its owner, and choose Temporary (with an end date) or Permanent.
  2. A second person approves it on Exceptions & Issues › Compensating Controls.
Proposed→Approved→Active→ExpiredWithdrawn

An approved, active compensating control counts towards residual risk. When a temporary one reaches its end date, Fortis expires it and flags the underlying exception again.

16.2Improvements

Exceptions & Issues › Improvements is the register of improvement actions from any source: exceptions, incidents, investigations, audits, self-assessments. Each action moves through:

Proposed→Approved→In Progress→Implemented→Verified

Verification is by someone other than the action's owner. Open improvements show on the control's page as Known improvements.

§ 17 · Risk

Risk management

Fortis keeps the risks your controls exist to reduce, so every rating and every exception can be read against the risk it affects.

17.1The risk register

Risk › Risk Register lists risks by category (a 12-branch taxonomy is provided), owner, entity and process, with inherent and residual ratings. Link a risk to its controls on the risk or control page; residual risk then moves with control ratings (8.5).

A risk assessment records dated inherent, residual and target positions, with a rationale for likelihood and impact. Impact is scored across six dimensions — financial, regulatory, reputational, operational, customer and people — and the driving dimension stays visible: a Critical regulatory impact is never averaged away. Above your review threshold, an assessment needs a second-line reviewer who is not the assessor before it is published.

For risks you can quantify, the simulation option runs a 10,000-iteration Monte Carlo model and returns expected loss, VaR at 95% and 99%, and a loss-exceedance curve. The result is reproducible.

17.2Heatmap

Risk › Risk Heatmap plots inherent, residual or target positions, or the movement from residual to target. Filter by category, entity, owner, process, framework or appetite breach, and click a cell to see the risks in it. Every cell shows its band name as well as its colour, and a table view gives the same figures without colour. The grid size, labels, bands and colours are set by your organisation.

17.3Risk appetite

Risk › Risk Appetite holds board-approved statements by category and entity, with tolerance bands and capacity. The author never approves a statement, and a change creates a new version rather than editing the old one. When a risk crosses tolerance it is flagged and escalated immediately.

17.4Treatment plans

Risk › Treatment Plans: choose Avoid, Reduce, Transfer, Accept or Exploit; add milestones, cost and expected benefit; track progress. Someone other than the owner verifies completion. Acceptance needs a Control Function Head and an expiry date.

17.5Key indicators

Risk › Key Indicators holds KRIs and KPIs with threshold bands. Values can be entered by hand, received from an integration, or calculated from other indicators. A Red or Critical reading opens a breach, escalates it, and — if your organisation chooses — raises a linked exception. Each indicator shows a trend chart with its thresholds.

17.6Objectives and strategy

Risk › Objectives and Strategy Map link strategic objectives to their measures, initiatives and risks. Progress rolls up nightly. An objective nothing measures shows as unmeasured, not as zero.

§ 18 · Incidents

Incidents and third parties

18.1Incidents

Risk › Incidents records operational events from first report to lessons learned.

Reported→Triaged→Under Investigation→Contained→Remediated→Closed→Reopened
  • Loss is recorded as gross loss and recovery; net loss is calculated. Each incident is tagged with its Basel event type. Near misses are recorded but kept out of loss totals.
  • Regulatory clocks. Fortis works out which notifications the incident triggers — for example the NDPC 72-hour breach notification — and shows a live countdown beside the legal reference.
  • Failed controls. Naming a control that failed flags it for re-testing this period and raises an exception against it.
  • Closure. An incident cannot close while a mandatory action or a regulatory notification is outstanding.

18.2Third parties

Risk › Third Parties holds your vendor and outsourcing relationships, their due-diligence reviews and contracts. An approved review expires on the relationship's own review cycle. When it lapses, Fortis raises an exception against the relationship. Contracts move into their renewal-notice window automatically, and a concentration view shows exposure by provider.

§ 19 · Compliance

Compliance

Fortis connects your controls to the regulations they satisfy, so one test can evidence many requirements and every filing deadline is in one calendar.

19.1Frameworks

Compliance › Frameworks shows each framework as a tree — domain, principle, control objective — with coverage per requirement and a coverage heatmap by entity. Frameworks are installed from content packs (26.6): international standards such as COSO and ISO, Nigerian regulator packs and pan-African packs.

Verification status

Every framework, requirement and obligation carries a verification status. Only verified records can enter a regulatory submission; others are badged Unverified wherever they appear. Your compliance team should verify Nigerian and African pack content against the regulator's primary document before relying on it.

19.2Mapping controls to requirements

On a requirement or a control, map the control to the requirement it satisfies. Fortis can suggest mappings and shows the shared terms behind each suggestion. A Control Officer maps; a Control Function Head approves. An unapproved mapping counts for nothing in coverage. Cross-framework equivalences mean one control's page lists every requirement it satisfies, directly or by inheritance: test once, satisfy many.

19.3Obligations and the compliance calendar

The Compliance › Obligation Register holds each regulatory duty: its due rule, who it applies to, its legal reference and its penalty. The Compliance Calendar shows every due instance by month, quarter or year, filtered by regulator, entity, owner and status. Reminders go out at 90, 60, 30, 14, 7, 3 and 1 days before the due date, on the day, and daily once overdue. Overdue instances show their live penalty exposure.

  • An instance reaches Submitted only with a submission reference and at least one evidence item. A second person accepts it.
  • Declaring an obligation not applicable takes effect only after a second person approves.

19.4Regulatory changes

Compliance › Regulatory Changes captures new circulars and publications, by hand or from regulator feeds polled each morning. Each moves through New → Under Review → Impact Assessed → Actioned, linked to the obligations and controls it affects. The assessor cannot sign it off as Actioned.

19.5Policies

Compliance › Policies manages policies, procedures, standards, guidelines, charters and codes.

Draft→Under Review→Pending Approval→Approved→Published→Under Revision→Superseded / Withdrawn

Each policy section names the controls and requirements it governs. Publishing supersedes the previous version, sets the next review date and, where required, opens an attestation campaign automatically. Policy gap analysis lists requirements no published policy governs. Waivers are time-boxed, never approved by the requester, and expire automatically.

§ 20 · Speak Up

Speak Up and cases

Speak Up is your organisation's whistleblowing channel. Anyone — staff, customers, suppliers — can report a concern without signing in, and can choose to stay anonymous.

20.1Reporting a concern

  1. Go to the /speak-up page of your Fortis address. No account is needed.
  2. Choose how you would like to report: Anonymous (Fortis stores nothing that identifies you) or Confidential (you give your name, and only the people handling the case see it). The page explains who can see the report under each option.
  3. Choose the type of concern and describe what happened, when, and who was involved.
  4. Submit. The confirmation page — Your report has been received — shows a reference and a token. Write both down. They are shown once and cannot be recovered.
  5. To follow up, go to Check a report, enter the reference and token, read any reply and Add more information.
How anonymity is protected

An anonymous report stores no reporter identity, and its audit trail records that events happened without recording who, from where or on which device. Only a one-way fingerprint of the token is stored, so a returning reporter can be recognised but never named. Reports arriving through a channel like WhatsApp have the sender's number stripped before they are saved.

20.2Handling a case

Reports land in Exceptions & Issues › Cases. A case is visible only to the people on its access list. When a report names nobody, Fortis gives it to the roles your organisation has set for intake (by default, the Control Function Head).

Received→Assessed→Under Investigation→Substantiated · Unsubstantiated · Referred→Closed
  • Record the triage decision, the investigation plan, privileged notes and the conclusion on the case.
  • Use Grant access to… under Who may see this case to add a colleague. Every grant and every file open is logged.
  • Mark a note as visible to the reporter to reply to them through their status page.
  • Where the matter needs formal investigation, raise an investigation from the case (Chapter 21). The case shows only the investigation's reference, status, risk rating and lead.
Rule

The case access list is absolute. There is no administrator override, and the Control Function Head reaches a case only by being named on it. The System Administrator holds read-only oversight, and every view is logged. Executives see only the board extract: counts and outcomes, never titles, people or notes.

20.3Reporter metadata and reveal approvals

For confidential (not anonymous) reports, Fortis keeps device and session signals in two tiers. Tier 1 is non-identifying and helps screen out false reports. Tier 2 — identifying data — is encrypted and sealed. To open it, a case handler clicks Request a reveal with a reason and a written justification. A second person holding reveal authority — often the DPO — decides it in Exceptions & Issues › Reveal Approvals. Nobody can approve their own request. Every access is written to an immutable metadata access log, and a legal hold can be set on the metadata.

§ 21 · Investigations

Investigations

An investigation is formal casework into suspected fraud, misconduct or a serious control breakdown. It can be opened on its own, from an exception, or from a Speak Up case.

21.1The investigation lifecycle

Draft→Reported→Under Investigation→Pending Review→Completed→Closed

An open investigation can be Suspended and later resumed. Pending Review can return to Under Investigation.

21.2Running an investigation

  1. Open Exceptions & Issues › Investigations and create one, or click Escalate to investigation on an exception. Record what is alleged, the category, priority, the entity or unit, the lead investigator and the target completion date, then click Open investigation.
  2. Build the team with Add a team member. Declare any segregation-of-duties conflict.
  3. Use Name a subject for each person under investigation, and Log a diary entry for every interview, request and step.
  4. Record each finding with Record a finding of fact, attach the evidence, and keep the Financial position current: estimated impact, confirmed loss and amount recovered.
  5. Recommend consequences for each subject under Consequence management, and track them with Mark in progress and Mark implemented.
  6. Write the report narrative and move the investigation to Pending Review with Move status.
  7. The Group Head Internal Control reviews the report version and clicks Approve or Reject, then issues it.
  8. Click Complete. Close and, when the retention point is reached, Archive the investigation.
Rule

An investigation cannot be completed without a risk rating, a completion date and a recorded outcome for every named subject. You cannot finish one without saying what happened to the people it named.

21.3Who can see an investigation

TypeVisible to
OrdinaryThe lead investigator, the team, the creator, and holders of view all investigations.
ConfidentialThe lead investigator, the team, and holders of view confidential investigations. Every view of the file is logged on the case timeline.

The Investigation Dashboard gives supervisors and executives the totals — open cases by status, priority and age, financial impact and recoveries — without the case content.

§ 22 · Complaints

Complaints

The complaints module handles customer complaints under the CBN Consumer Protection Framework, with the regulatory clocks running from the moment a complaint is logged.

  1. Log the complaint in Exceptions & Issues › Complaints with the customer, channel and detail. Fortis issues the customer a tracking ID.
  2. Acknowledge it. The 24-hour acknowledgement clock is read from the CBN obligation record, and the acknowledgement time is stamped by the server — it cannot be back-dated.
  3. Assign it, record progress and, if it reveals an operational event, raise an incident from it.
  4. Click Resolve the complaint. A root cause is mandatory; record the resolution summary, outcome and any redress.
  5. Close it. A complaint can be reopened or escalated.

Each open complaint shows its live penalty exposure: the acknowledgement penalty if that window was missed, plus the weekly penalty for every started week past the resolution deadline (14 days unless your organisation sets another). Analysis groups complaints by root cause and links them to the failing control. The CBN CPD return exports to Excel.

§ 23 · Evidence

Evidence and documents

Evidence is what proves a test, a remediation or a finding. Fortis keeps it so that it can be produced to an examiner, and disposes of it when the law says it should go.

23.1Uploading evidence

  1. On a test, exception or finding, open the Evidence panel and choose the file. On a phone you can take a photo directly.
  2. Declare whether the file contains customer personal data. If it does, select the categories. You cannot upload until you answer.
  3. Read the data-minimisation prompt. Upload only what proves the point: a masked account number is usually enough.
  4. Upload. Fortis stores the file outside the public web folder with a checksum that proves it has not changed.

Every view and download is logged with the user and time. Files are served only to people allowed to see the record.

23.2Retention, legal hold and disposal

  • Retention. Your DPO sets retention periods. Each item's expiry is calculated from them.
  • Disposal. Each night, expired evidence enters the disposal queue at Evidence › Evidence Disposal. Two different control function approvers must approve each disposal. The disposal log cannot be changed.
  • Legal hold. Evidence under legal hold is never disposed of, whatever its retention date.

23.3Documents

Evidence › Documents holds governing documents — procedure manuals, charters, terms of reference — separately from evidence. Documents sit in a folder tree, are approved by someone other than their owner, are published with a supersession chain, and carry a review date. Owners are reminded on Mondays when a review falls due within 30 days. Confidential documents are restricted to named roles.

§ 25 · AI

AI assistance

Fortis includes an assistant, Atlas, and drafting aids on many forms. They run on a model hosted inside your organisation's environment. They help you write and find things; they never decide.

  • Where you see it. Draft with AI on control and policy text, Suggest root cause on exceptions, mapping suggestions on frameworks, and the Atlas chat panel.
  • A draft is a draft. AI output becomes part of a record only when a named person accepts it. The acceptance is recorded.

26.5Integrations and data sources

  • Integrations — connect Fortis to Atheris thirdLine (internal audit) and Atheris Vantage (ERM). Choose which system is master for control definitions. Fortis publishes controls on approval, test results on sign-off and exceptions on closure; every attempt is logged and failed ones can be replayed. Other systems use the Fortis API with a per-organisation key.
  • Data Sources — connect core banking, ERP, payments and tax systems for continuous monitoring. Each source has its own rate limit, timeout and circuit breaker, must be approved before it runs, and stores extracts only in your country's data plane.

26.6Bulk import and content packs

  • Bulk Import — load controls, risks, obligations, organisation units and users from Excel. Download the template, fill it in, and run a dry run that reports errors row by row without writing anything. The import itself is all-or-nothing.
  • Content Packs — install regulatory frameworks and obligations: international standards, Nigerian regulators and pan-African regulators. Each install shows what it will change first and never overwrites your own records.
  • AI Governance — which AI capabilities are enabled, prompt versions, usage budgets and the AI interaction log.
